# Round 2 — English demand research (audience: English-searching buyers inside Malaysia)

Researched live on **2026-08-25** by repeated SERP sampling (`WebSearch`/`WebFetch`). There is no
keyword-volume tool on this machine — Search Console quota is exhausted and no paid tool is
available — so **no monthly search-volume figure appears anywhere in this file**. Demand is
reported as observable evidence only: how many competing pages exist, what type they are, how thin
or strong the ranking set is, and the exact question phrasings that surfaced in result titles and
snippets. Every claim below carries the URL it came from.

## What this round is writing against

**Already taken by Round 1 (2026-08-20) — no candidate below targets these or a close variant:**

| `how to identify pure honey` | authenticity tests, blended-vs-pure, "crystallisation is a good sign" |

The last one is the tightest fence again this round: it already argues that variation and
crystallisation are *not* proof of fakery. Any new candidate that touches authenticity, testing, or
"is this real" must stay off that ground entirely — see candidate 9 below for how that boundary is
drawn.

Round 1 also left five candidates **researched but not commissioned** (reserves, never published):
`what does raw honey mean`, `does hot water destroy honey`, `honey for kids what age`,
`honey gift malaysia`, `how much honey per day`. These are not in the banned list — no page targets
them — but they are already fully evidenced in `docs/press/round-1/demand-en.md`, so this round does
not re-research them. If a future round wants them, that file is the source, not this one.

**Facts the site does not publish** (price, jar size, rating, customer count, certification, country
of origin, shelf life, nutritional values). Every candidate below is answerable without any of them.

**Legal ceiling**, confirmed again for this round: Malaysia's Food Regulations 1985 bar a food and
its advertising from claiming to prevent, reduce, treat or cure a condition
(<https://www.foodipedia.my/forbidden-claims-on-product-labels-in-malaysia/>,
<https://www.foodipedia.my/food-regulations-1985.pdf>). No fertility promises, no medical verbs, no
dosage-as-cure framing. Two candidates this round (face mask, batch-taste-variation) sit closer to
that line than anything Round 1 shipped, and are flagged accordingly.

  the American Pregnancy Association and the Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists all

  diagnosed. Phrasings observed: *"can you eat honey while pregnant"*, *"is raw honey safe during
  pregnancy"*, *"honey and pregnancy safety"*.
- **Competition:** dense, foreign, medically sourced — but **no Malaysian page exists**, despite this
  being exactly the kind of question a Malaysian mother-to-be or her family would ask before buying a
  jar as a gift or for daily use.
- **Angle SUBUR can own honestly:** report the medical consensus plainly, cite the health bodies the
  way the foreign sources do, and correct the common confusion with the infant rule (a searcher who
  half-remembers "no honey for babies" may wrongly extend it to pregnancy). This is safety
  information sourced from public health authorities, not a claim the product makes about itself.
- **Related keywords, grouped by intent:**
  - *product:* raw honey, honeycomb honey
  - *usage:* honey while pregnant, honey in pregnancy diet, eating honey trimester
  - *buying:* —
  - *comparison:* honey vs raw egg pregnancy safety, pasteurised vs raw honey pregnancy
  - *trust:* is honey safe during pregnancy, can pregnant women eat honey, honey and gestational diabetes, why babies can't have honey but adults can
- **Risk:** the gestational-diabetes caveat must be reported as existing public-health advice
  ("consult your own doctor if diagnosed"), never rewritten as SUBUR advising on a medical condition.
  No claim that honey benefits pregnancy in any way — this article answers a safety question, it does
  not promote consumption.

### 7. Honey allergy — who should be careful, and why

- **Focus keyword:** `honey allergy symptoms`
- **Intent:** informational / trust (safety, pre-purchase caution)
- **Demand evidence:** consistent global demand, dense but scattered:
  <https://www.wyndly.com/blogs/learn/honey-allergy>,
  <https://intolerancelab.co.uk/honey-allergy-symptoms-and-effective-management/>,
  <https://sudhahoney.com/blogs/honey-allergy-symptoms>. Sources agree true honey allergy is rare
  (under 0.2% of the population) and describe symptoms from mild hives to rare anaphylaxis, plus a
  separate clinical thread on pollen cross-reactivity — patients sensitised to weed or Compositae
  (daisy-family) pollen can react to honey containing that pollen, and pollen-allergic people show
  elevated Hymenoptera (bee/wasp) venom sensitisation without a sting history
  (<https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7478646/>,
  <https://www.jacionline.org/article/S0091-6749%252896%252970284-1/fulltext>,
  <https://www.beeawareallergy.com/bee-stings/allergy-testing/allergic-to-honey/>). One Malaysian
  research signal exists but is off-topic for this angle — a randomised trial using honey *for*
  allergic rhinitis symptoms
  (<https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258280802_Ingestion_of_honey_improves_the_symptoms_of_allergic_rhinitis_Evidence_from_a_randomized_placebo-controlled_trial_in_the_East_Coast_of_Peninsular_Malaysia>)
  — that is a treatment claim and must not be cited as if it supports this article.
- **Competition:** foreign allergy-clinic and supplement-seller pages, no Malaysian page, and no page
  written from a honey seller's side that plainly tells a buyer what to watch for.
- **Angle SUBUR can own honestly:** a plain safety note — what a true honey allergy looks like, that
  it's rare, why pollen-sensitive people specifically should be cautious, and that a comb-and-nut
  blend adds a second, more common allergen (tree nuts) on top of the honey question. Purely
  cautionary and factual; this is the kind of content a responsible seller publishes, not a benefit
  claim.
- **Related keywords, grouped by intent:**
  - *product:* raw honey, honeycomb, bee pollen, honey with nuts
  - *usage:* —
  - *buying:* —
  - *comparison:* honey allergy vs pollen allergy, honey allergy vs bee sting allergy
  - *trust:* honey allergy symptoms, can you be allergic to honey, is honey allergy common, honey and nut allergy, honey and pollen allergy
- **Risk:** the Malaysian source found is a treatment-claim study (honey *for* rhinitis) — it must be
  excluded from the article entirely, not softened. The article's job is caution, not therapeutic
  framing in either direction. Anaphylaxis must be described plainly as "seek medical attention," not
  minimised.
