# Round 1 — English demand research (audience: English-searching buyers inside Malaysia)

Researched live on **2026-08-20** by repeated SERP sampling. There is no keyword tool on this
machine (`PROJECT-PROFILE.md`, capability probe) and Search Console is unavailable, so **no monthly
volume figure appears anywhere in this file**. Demand is reported as observable evidence only:
how many competing Malaysian pages exist, what type they are, how thin they are, and the exact
question phrasings that surfaced in result titles and snippets.

## What this round is writing against

**Already taken — no candidate below targets these or a near-duplicate:**

| Live EN article | Focus it owns | What that closes off |
|---|---|---|
| `honey-for-couples-malaysia` | madu suami isteri / couples honey | couples, marriage, fertility-adjacent framing |
| `black-seed-honey-benefits` | habbatus sauda + honey / black seed honey | black seed, Sunnah-trio, kurma pairing |
| `how-to-identify-pure-honey` | cara kenal madu asli / real vs fake home tests | thumb-water-burn tests, adulteration, **and it already contains a "blended vs pure" section and a "crystallisation is a good sign" line** |

That third one is the tightest fence. Any authenticity or adulteration angle is spent. Trust-family
candidates below therefore attack **processing, provenance and storage**, not "is it fake".

**Facts the site does not publish** (price, jar size, rating, customer count, certification,
country of origin, shelf life, nutritional values). Every candidate below is answerable without a
single one of them; where a reader would want one, the article's honest move is to send them to
the label or the shop's WhatsApp.

**Legal ceiling.** Malaysia's Food Regulations 1985 bar a food and its advertising from claiming to
prevent, reduce, treat or cure a condition — confirmed 2026-08-20 against
<https://www.foodipedia.my/forbidden-claims-on-product-labels-in-malaysia/> and the consolidated
text at <https://www.foodipedia.my/food-regulations-1985.pdf>. Enforcement is real, not theoretical
(Malay Mail, 26 Nov 2019: 47 notices issued to premises over health claims on food labels —
<https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2019/11/26/health-claims-on-food-labels-47-notices-issued-to-owners-of-premises/1813263>).
So the large "honey for X condition" demand is only servable by writing *about* the search, not by
answering it as a claim. Each candidate carries a Risk line saying where its cliff edge is.

### 2. Can you eat the wax? How comb honey is actually eaten

- **Focus keyword:** `can you eat honeycomb wax`
- **Intent:** informational (post-purchase, high intent to return)
- **Demand evidence:** a stable, heavily-answered question globally — the SERP returns a full page
  of dedicated articles, which is itself the demand signal: Manukora
  (<https://manukora.com/blogs/honey-guide/can-you-eat-honeycomb>), Ames Farm publishes **two**
  separate pages on it (<https://www.amesfarm.com/blogs/honeycomb/can-you-eat-honeycomb> and
  <https://www.amesfarm.com/blogs/honeycomb/can-you-eat-honeycomb-everything-you-need-to-know>),
  Bee Inspired (<https://beeinspiredgoods.com/blogs/honey-recipes/eating-honeycomb>), Honey Heaven
  (<https://www.honeyheaven.co.uk/blogs/news/can-you-eat-wax-honeycomb>), Scottish Bee Company
  (<https://www.scottishbeecompany.co.uk/blogs/news/top-facts-about-honeycomb>), plus a long-running
  Quora thread (<https://www.quora.com/Can-humans-eat-Honeycomb>). Question phrasings observed
  verbatim in titles and snippets: *"can you eat honeycomb"*, *"can you eat the wax on honeycomb"*,
  *"can humans eat honeycomb"*, *"do you swallow the wax or spit it out"*, *"is honeycomb safe to eat"*.
- **Competition:** dense but **entirely US/UK**. Not one Malaysian page in the sampled results,
  despite Malaysian marketplaces selling comb by the kilo (see candidate 1). Foreign pages also all
  pivot to selling Manuka or US comb, and all describe eating comb on a cheese board — the wrong
  serving context for the local buyer.
- **Angle SUBUR can own honestly:** the only Malaysian page answering it, written for a jar where
  the comb sits in a spiced blend rather than in a clean square — how the wax behaves at room
  temperature in a Malaysian kitchen versus in an air-conditioned one, what chewing it is like,
  and that swallowing or discarding the wax is a preference, not a rule.
- **Related keywords, grouped by intent:**

  - *usage:* can you eat honeycomb wax, how to eat honeycomb, chew honeycomb like gum, honeycomb in tea, honeycomb on toast
  - *buying:* honeycomb honey malaysia
  - *comparison:* honeycomb vs honey texture
  - *trust:* is beeswax safe to swallow, honeycomb and children under one
- **Risk:** the under-one-year-old botulism point must be stated as the standing safety rule it is,
  not as a product warning that implies the product is otherwise a remedy. Allergy caveat likewise
  — describe, do not advise.

### 4. Kelulut, Tualang, wild, blended — a map of the honey types actually sold in Malaysia

- **Focus keyword:** `types of honey in malaysia`
- **Intent:** informational / comparison (pillar-shaped; links to most other candidates)
- **Demand evidence:** heavy demand, and the top of the SERP is **academic**, which is a classic gap
  signal — a shopper's question being answered by journals. Sampled 2026-08-20: MDPI *Nutrients*
  review of Tualang, Gelam and Kelulut honeys (<https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/1/197>), Current
  Research in Nutrition and Food Science comparison of Kelulut and Tualang
  (<https://www.foodandnutritionjournal.org/volume14number2/comprehensive-assessment-of-physicochemical-characteristics-mineral-content-and-antioxidant-capacity-of-malaysian-kelulut-and-tualang-honeys/>),
  ScienceDirect LC-MS comparison (<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0023643817307582>).
  The consumer-side results are seller blogs: BeeBudz "3 Local Honey That Can Only Be Found In
  Malaysia" (<https://beebudzhq.com/3-local-honey-that-can-only-be-found-in-malaysia/>), Palate Asia

  - *trust:* what is madu hutan, why kelulut costs more
- **Risk:** the entire academic literature on these honeys is written in the language of therapeutic
  effect (wound healing, antioxidant capacity, antibacterial action). Citing any of it as a product
  property breaks the ceiling. The article must stay on **taste, texture, moisture, colour, how it
  is produced and what it costs to produce** — sensory and process facts only. Also: SUBUR publishes
  no origin, so the article must not imply SUBUR's honey is any named Malaysian varietal.

### 5. Storing honey in Malaysian heat and humidity — crystals, foam, ants and the fridge question

- **Focus keyword:** `how to store honey malaysia`

  a tropical-specific storage page ranks; its Singapore framing leaves Malaysia open. Supporting
  demand across three distinct sub-questions — crystallisation
  (<https://beekeepercorner.com/when-does-honey-crystallize/>,
  <https://scienceline.org/2007/04/ask-westly-crystallizedhoney/>), fermentation and foam
  (<https://rawhoneyguide.com/learn/does-honey-expire>, <https://carolinahoneybees.com/honey-go-bad/>,

  expire"*, *"why is my honey foamy"*, *"can I keep honey in the fridge"*, *"how to keep ants out of
