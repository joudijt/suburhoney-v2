## 0. How to read the evidence in this file (read before the candidates)

**There are no search-volume figures anywhere in this file.** No paid keyword tool is on this
machine, Cogny's free quota is exhausted, and Google Ads Keyword Planner was not used. Every number
below is one of exactly two kinds, and each is labelled where it appears:

1. **GSC impressions / clicks / average position** — real, from `gsc-demand.md`, pulled
   2026-08-25 from `sc-domain:madinah.com.my` over a 16-month window. This is the *retailer's*
   property, not SUBUR's. It proves **what this audience types**. It does not prove SUBUR is the
   answer, and it does not tell us the size of the Malaysian market for that phrase — a query with
   1,935 impressions on madinah.com.my is a query madinah.com.my happened to surface for, nothing
   more.
2. **SERP composition** — who actually ranks today, how long their pages are, what framing they
   use. Every one of these carries a URL and the pull date 2026-08-25. Where I say a corpus is
   "entirely health-claim content", that is my reading of the titles and extracts returned on
   2026-08-25, not a claim about page bodies I did not open.

`suburhoney.com` itself has **zero query rows** in 16 months (1 page, 12 impressions on `/en/`).
There is no harvest to make from our own property. Round 3 stays demand-led, exactly like rounds 1
and 2.

Two further named exclusions that a reviewer will otherwise ask about:

- **`honey for cough` / `honey for sore throat`.** Very large global demand, and unusually, real

- **`is honey halal` / `madu halal Malaysia`.** Large Malaysian demand. **Disqualified on

  all high-intent, and all **disqualified: SUBUR publishes no price.** A price-comparison article

### 0.2 Banned-terms note

Three candidates below sit near the banned-term line and are flagged individually. The general
finding: the sidr/Yemeni space is where `Middle East` wants to appear, because every competitor

rather than the region — but the writer must be briefed explicitly or the gate will fail the build.
No candidate in this file *requires* a banned term to be honest. If one had, I would have cut it.

## Candidate 1 — `is honey in malaysia fake`

- **Focus keyword:** `is honey in malaysia fake`
- **Slug:** `is-honey-in-malaysia-fake`
- **Intent:** informational — claim-verification. The searcher has seen the "90% of honey in
  Malaysia is fake" line on TikTok or in a WhatsApp forward and wants to know if it is true.

**Demand evidence**

- GSC (madinah.com.my, 16mo, pulled 2026-08-25): `al shifa honey is pure or not` — **262
  impressions**, position 3.0, 5 clicks. `is al shifa honey pure` — **101 impressions**, position
  5.7. `madu al shifa palsu` — 30 impressions, position 1.5. `perbedaan madu al shifa asli dan
  palsu` — 18 impressions, position 2.1. `madu al shifa asli dan palsu` — 9 impressions.
  `al shifa honey quality` — 1. That is **~420 impressions of pure "is this honey fake"
  verification intent**, concentrated on a competitor brand, and it is the second-largest
  *legal* cluster in the file after sidr/Yemeni.
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25: the claim is a live Malaysian news story with a clean, citable
  government counter-datum on the record.
  - `https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/02/13/armizan-ministry-looking-into-claim-that-nine-in-10-honey-products-here-are-fake/117739` — KPDN minister Armizan Mohd Ali responds to the claim, 13 Feb 2024. (Malay Mail returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch; the headline, date and substance are confirmed by three independent syndications below.)
  - `https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/02/14/amid-claim-that-nine-in-10-honey-products-are-fake-health-ministry-says-its-monitoring-shows-only-6pc-not-up-to-standard/117955` — **the payoff**: MOH says its own monitoring since 2016 found only ~6% of honey products did not meet standard.
  - `https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2024/02/13/armizan-says-ministry-to-investigate-claim-90-of-honey-products-in-malaysia-potentially-fake/`
  - `https://mustsharenews.com/malaysian-lecturer-honey-fake/` — attributes the original claim to Dr Zulkifli Mustafa, senior lecturer, School of Medical Sciences, Universiti Sains Malaysia.
  - `https://says.com/my/news/moh-has-now-addressed-the-claim-that-90-of-honey-sold-in-malaysia-is-fake`
  - `https://e.vnexpress.net/news/business/companies/expert-claims-90-of-honey-in-malaysia-is-artificial-4711335.html` — the claim travelled internationally, which is why it keeps resurfacing.

**What currently ranks**

Two shapes, and neither is the article. (a) **News reports**, 300–600 words, each covering one
day of the story — the claim on the 13th, the rebuttal on the 14th — and *none of them puts the
two together*. (b) **Seller listicles** — `says.com/my/makan/difference-between-genuine-fake-honey`
and `natures-glory.com/blogs/news/real-honey-vs-fake-honey-in-malaysia`, both of which *repeat the
90% figure as established fact* and then pivot to the home-test list (thumb test, water test,
crystallisation) that our own `how-to-identify-pure-honey` already demonstrates is unreliable.
**The gap:** nobody has written the piece that says "here is where the number came from, here is
what the Health Ministry's own monitoring says instead, and here is why the difference is a
definitions problem, not a cover-up."

**The angle**

This is a fact-check, not a how-to. The 16 live articles include `cara kenal madu asli`
(`how-to-identify-pure-honey`), which teaches a reader to evaluate a jar. This article does the
opposite job: it evaluates a *statistic*. Named source, named counter-source, both on the public
record, with the honest resolution — a lecturer's sampling remark and a ministry's compliance
monitoring are measuring different things, and "fake" is doing a lot of unexamined work in
between. It ends by handing the reader off to the existing testing guide rather than re-running it.
For a brand with no certifications and no lab results to publish, a piece whose entire authority
comes from *other people's* published numbers is unusually well-suited: SUBUR contributes the
reading, not the data.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* **low.** No health claim is available or needed. One watch-out: do not let the
  piece drift into "so buy from a trusted seller like us" — that converts a fact-check into an
  advertisement claim about competitor safety.
- *Fabrication:* **low, with one hard rule.** The 6% and the 90% must be attributed to MOH and to
  Dr Zulkifli Mustafa/USM by name and reproduced exactly as reported. Do not compute a derived
  figure ("so 94% is fine"), do not state a sample size neither party published, and do not assert
  the claim was retracted — as of this pull it was disputed, not withdrawn. **Do not imply SUBUR's
  own jars were tested.** They were not, and nothing about SUBUR's testing status is published.
- *Cannibalisation:* **medium — the one to manage.** `how-to-identify-pure-honey` is the nearest
  neighbour and this article must not restate its test list. Boundary: that article answers *"is my
  jar real?"*; this one answers *"is the 90% number real?"*. The brief must forbid a home-test
  section outright and require an internal link instead.
- *Banned terms:* **low.** Nothing in this topic pulls toward the banned list.

**Cross-language:** Strong Malay demand (`madu palsu Malaysia` is the native phrasing, and GSC
already shows Malay/Indonesian `asli dan palsu` variants with clicks). Moderate Arabic demand —
GSC has `هل عسل الشفاء طبيعي` at 23 impressions position 2.6 and `عسل الشفاء هل هو طبيعي` at 17.
**Group all three.**

## Candidate 2 — `what is sidr honey`

- **Focus keyword:** `what is sidr honey`
- **Slug:** `what-is-sidr-honey`
- **Intent:** informational, with a strong commercial tail (the searcher is usually deciding
  whether to buy some).

**Demand evidence**

- GSC (madinah.com.my, 16mo, pulled 2026-08-25) — **this is by a wide margin the largest legal
  cluster in the file**: `yemeni honey` **1,935 impressions** (position 11.5); `yemeni sidr honey`
  **1,801** (14.4); `yemeni honey ingredients` **207** (5.0); `sidr honey madinah` **194** (8.7);
  `madu madinah` **155** (2.2); `honey sidr yemen` **150** (9.3); `sidr honey price` 111;
  `yemeni honey price` 93; `yemeni honey near me` 44; `arabic honey` 37; `all fruits for yemeni
  honey` 33; `yemeni honey mixed for married` 30; `sidr yemeni honey` 30; `arab honey` 27;
  `yaman honey` 22; `yemeni honey benefits` 22; plus roughly 25 further long-tail rows.
  Conservatively **~4,700 impressions**, and the two head terms sit at **position 11.5 and 14.4** —
  page two. This audience is asking, and the retailer is not answering well.
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25:
  - `https://sedra.com.my/products/yemeni-sidr-honey` — Malaysian seller, product page.
  - `https://www.mahnazfood.com/products/mahnaz-food-lale-sidr-honey-lale-madu-sidr-220g-420g` — Malaysian seller, product page, uses "madu sidr".
  - `https://kashmiril.com/blogs/journal/sidr-honey-vs-regular-honey-why-scientists-are-stunned`
  - `https://purewhites.in/blogs/our-blogs/sidr-honey-vs-regular-honey-what-s-the-difference`
  - `https://littlehoneystraws.com/sidr-honey-best-in-world/`
  - `https://cso-yemen.org/sidr-honey/`
  - `https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212429226005663` — a 2026 review paper, paywalled abstract.

**What currently ranks**

The Malaysian results are **product pages, not articles** — a title, a price, a paragraph. The
editorial results are all foreign seller blogs, roughly 1,200–2,500 words, and every one of them is
built on claims SUBUR cannot make: "antibacterial, antioxidant, anti-diabetic, and cancer-fighting
properties", "medicinal", "Scientists Are Stunned". **The gap is a Malaysian explainer with no
claims in it at all** — what the Ziziphus spina-christi tree is, why the flowering window makes the
honey scarce, what the honey physically looks and tastes like, why the price is what it is, and
what the buyer is actually paying for. Nobody in Malaysia has written that.

**The angle**

The honest version of this article is the one that says, in the second paragraph, **"SUBUR is not
sidr honey."** That is the whole point of writing it. This audience walks into Berkat Madinah
looking for sidr and finds a shelf that also carries a ten-ingredient comb blend; right now nothing
explains the difference, so the two products blur. The article defines sidr as a *monofloral*
honey — one tree, one nectar, one short season — and then draws the contrast that SUBUR's own
category needs drawn: a comb blend is a **different product class**, bought for the whole spoonful
rather than for one flower, with **no floral source published for it at all**. That contrast is
already the settled house position — `types-of-honey-in-malaysia` makes exactly this argument
against kelulut and tualang. This article extends it to the one honey the existing 16 never
mention, and which the GSC data says is what the audience actually types.

**How it stays honest — spelled out, as required:**
1. SUBUR is named as *not* sidr, early and in plain words. No "similar to", no "our answer to".
2. No sidr health claim is repeated, even attributed. Descriptive only: colour, viscosity, flavour,
   scarcity, price pressure.
3. No claim that SUBUR *contains* sidr, and no claim about SUBUR's floral source, because none is
   published.
4. Sidr's country of origin is named as **Yemen and Saudi Arabia** — never the banned regional
   shorthand.
5. No comparison of quality or grade between sidr and SUBUR. Different product classes do not rank
   against each other; saying so is the article's thesis.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* **medium — the highest in the recommended six.** The entire incumbent corpus is
  medical claims and the writer will feel the pull. Brief must forbid: antibacterial, antioxidant,
  anti-diabetic, anti-inflammatory, medicinal, healing, immunity — including as reported speech
  ("sellers say it is antibacterial" is still the claim appearing on our domain).
- *Fabrication:* **medium.** Do not publish a sidr price, a SUBUR price, or a comparison of the
  two — no price exists on our side. Do not assert SUBUR's shelf-neighbours or that Berkat Madinah
  stocks sidr; the site publishes a catalogue, not a stock list. Do not invent a harvest yield or
  a tonnage figure.
- *Cannibalisation:* **low.** Verified against all 16 live focus keywords. `types-of-honey-in-
  malaysia` enumerates kelulut, tualang, gelam, wild/forest, imported multifloral and blends, and
  **does not mention sidr or Yemen anywhere**; `manuka-honey-vs-local-honey` is a grading-scheme
  piece (MGO/UMF) and sidr has no grading scheme. Boundary: this article must not re-list Malaysian
  honey types — link to that article instead.
- *Banned terms:* **medium — flag it in the brief.** `Middle East` is the natural shorthand every
  competitor uses. Countries only.

**Cross-language:** Very strong in all three. Arabic is the obvious lead (this is the Arabic-
speaking-in-Malaysia audience's own product) and GSC carries Arabic-script rows. Malay demand is
evidenced by the Malaysian sellers ranking with `madu sidr`. **Group all three.**

## Candidate 3 — `can you put honey in hot tea`

- **Focus keyword:** `can you put honey in hot tea`
- **Slug:** `honey-in-hot-drinks-malaysia`
- **Intent:** informational, everyday-use. Often anxious — a large share of this traffic has heard
  that hot honey is *harmful*, not merely wasteful.

**Demand evidence**

- GSC: **weak and honest about it.** `benefits of drinking honey water` — 1 impression;
  `benefits of honey water` — 1. That is all the madinah.com.my log offers. This candidate is
  carried by SERP evidence, not by GSC, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25 — a dense, mature international corpus, which is itself the demand proof
  (nobody writes eight competing pages against a question nobody asks):
  - `https://shopbeecharmer.com/blogs/knowledge-inspiration/does-adding-honey-to-hot-tea-or-coffee-destroy-its-benefits`
  - `https://kashmiril.com/blogs/journal/does-hot-water-destroy-honey`
  - `https://beeinspiredgoods.com/blogs/good-reads/what-temperature-is-too-hot-for-honey`
  - `https://justbeehoney.co.uk/blogs/just-bee-honey-blog/can-i-mix-honey-with-hot-water`
  - `https://livebeekeeping.com/honey/honey-tea-benefits/` — the only one that names HMF.
  - `https://www.champlainvalleyhoney.com/blogs/all-posts/can-i-use-raw-honey-in-my-hot-tea-or-coffee`
  - `https://www.beesandtrees.com/blogs/news/manuka-honey-hot-water-benefits`
  - The corpus explicitly documents the myth it exists to answer: "It doesn't turn the honey
    'toxic' or harmful (a common myth)" (`shopbeecharmer.com`, pulled 2026-08-25).

**What currently ranks**

Eight foreign seller blogs, 800–1,800 words, all answering in **Fahrenheit**, all citing a
different threshold (104°F / 110°F / 40°C / 49°C — they do not agree with each other), and all
framing the payoff as *preserving benefits*. **Zero Malaysian results.** Nobody writes it for a
kitchen where the default hot drink is teh or kopi from a kettle that was boiling forty seconds
ago, and — decisively — **nobody writes it for a jar with wax and ground nuts in it.**

**The angle**

Two things make this SUBUR's rather than anyone's. First, the whole article can be written in
metric, against Malaysian drinks, with the practical answer being a wait time rather than a
thermometer reading. Second, and this is the part no competitor *can* write: a comb-and-nut blend
behaves differently in a hot cup. Beeswax does not dissolve, it softens and floats — consistent
with the density facts the site already publishes — and overheated wax goes cloudy and greasy,
which is the same warning `how-to-store-honey-malaysia` gives about warming a hardened jar, applied
to a mug instead. So the honest recommendation is not the incumbents' "wait for benefits", it is
"a comb blend is a spoon product; if you want honey in your teh, this is not the jar for it" —
a piece of advice that costs the brand a sale and buys the reader's trust. That refusal is the
article.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* **low-medium.** The incumbent framing ("preserves benefits") is a claim. Recast
  as chemistry and sensory: enzymes and aroma compounds are heat-labile, HMF rises with heat, the
  flavour flattens. Describe what changes in the honey; never state what that does for a person.
- *Fabrication:* **low.** One rule: pick **one** temperature threshold, attribute it, and note the
  sources disagree — do not average four blogs into a made-up number, and do not publish a
  temperature for SUBUR's own jar, which has never been measured.
- *Cannibalisation:* **low-medium.** `how-to-store-honey-malaysia` owns warming a *jar*
  (warm-water bath, never the microwave) and lists `how to soften crystallised honey`. Boundary:
  this article is about honey going **into a drink**, not about rescuing a set jar. The brief must
  forbid a jar-softening section and require the link.
- *Banned terms:* **low.**

**Cross-language:** Strong Malay (`bolehkah madu dicampur air panas` is a live Malay/Indonesian
question — GSC even carries the Indonesian-shaped `bolehkah cuka apel dicampur madu` at 1
impression, same construction). Moderate Arabic. **Group EN + MS at minimum.**

## Candidate 4 — `baking with honey instead of sugar`

- **Focus keyword:** `baking with honey instead of sugar`
- **Slug:** `baking-with-honey-malaysia`
- **Intent:** informational / practical, task-in-hand. The searcher has a recipe open.

**Demand evidence**

- GSC: **none.** madinah.com.my has no baking or cooking rows at all. Stated plainly: this
  candidate has **zero GSC support** and rests entirely on SERP composition. It is here because the
  competitive gap is unusually clean, not because the retailer's log vouches for it.
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25:
  - `https://eatbeautiful.net/exact-conversion-chart-sugar-honey-when-baking/`
  - `https://norcalnectar.com/blog/baking-with-raw-honey`
  - `https://www.weekshoneyfarm.com/blogs/recipes/how-to-replace-sugar-with-raw-honey-in-everyday-cooking`
  - `https://brightland.co/blogs/field-notes/baking-with-honey`
  - `https://qldhoneyandhives.com.au/honey-for-baking-sugar-substitution-tips/`
  - `https://kashmiril.com/blogs/journal/honey-as-sugar-substitute`
  - Consensus conversion in the corpus (pulled 2026-08-25): ¾ cup + 1 tbsp honey per 1 cup sugar;
    reduce other liquid by ¼ cup per cup of honey; add ¼ tsp baking soda; drop the oven by 25°F.

**What currently ranks**

US and Australian honey farms, 900–2,000 words, **every single one in cups and Fahrenheit**. A
Malaysian home baker working from a metric recipe and an oven marked in Celsius has to convert two
systems before the advice is usable. No Malaysian result ranks. And every one of them assumes a
pourable liquid honey — none addresses a blend with wax, ground nuts and whole spices in it.

**The angle**

A metric conversion table is the hook, but the article's real content is the honest boundary: **do
not bake with this jar.** A comb blend's wax will not behave in a batter, its ground walnut and
almond change the crumb, and its saffron, star anise, cinnamon, nutmeg and ginger will fight
whatever you are making. The article says so, tells the reader which honey *is* the cheap right
tool for baking (mild filtered multifloral — a point `types-of-honey-in-malaysia` already makes:
"heat flattens flavour anyway"), and then makes the positive case that costs nothing: a spiced comb
blend belongs *on* the finished thing, not *in* it — drizzled over a cooled bake, not creamed into
the batter. It is the same shape as candidate 3: the brand ruling itself out of a use case in
public. Nothing among the 16 live articles does cooking or baking at all — the nearest,
`honey-vs-gula-melaka`, is a flavour-and-role comparison, not a substitution method.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* **low.** This is the safest topic in the file — ratios and oven temperatures
  carry no claim. **One trap:** the incumbents pitch honey as the *healthier* sugar. That is a
  ceiling violation and must be cut; the honest reasons to bake with honey are moisture, browning
  and flavour.
- *Fabrication:* **low.** Convert the published ratios to metric and attribute them; do not invent
  a Malaysian-specific ratio, and do not publish a sugar content or calorie figure for SUBUR's jar
  (none exists — no nutrition panel is published).
- *Cannibalisation:* **low.** `honey-vs-gula-melaka` is the neighbour; it owns "which sweetener for
  which dish". This one owns "how to swap, by weight and by oven". Note for the round map: the MS
  reserve `gula melaka vs madu` was discarded in round 2 for a strong incumbent and a glycaemic-
  framing SERP — **that reason does not transfer here.** This SERP has no glycaemic framing and no
  strong Malaysian incumbent, because it has no Malaysian incumbent at all.
- *Banned terms:* **low.**

**Cross-language:** Moderate Malay (Malaysian home baking is a large content space and the metric
problem is identical). Weak Arabic. **EN + MS.**

## Candidate 5 — `how to read a honey label`

- **Focus keyword:** `how to read a honey label`
- **Slug:** `how-to-read-a-honey-label-malaysia`
- **Intent:** informational, pre-purchase, standing in the aisle with a jar in hand.

**Demand evidence**

- GSC (madinah.com.my, 16mo, pulled 2026-08-25) — real, and specifically about *what is in the
  jar*: `yemeni honey ingredients` **207 impressions** (position 5.0); `al shifa honey ingredients`
  3 (position 1.0); `al shifa honey country of origin` 5; `al shifa honey origin` 4; `al shifa
  honey made in which country` 1; `al shifa honey company` 1; `alshifa honey 1kg` 15;
  `al shifa natural honey 500gm` 2; `250gm honey` 5; `7kg natural honey container` 1. Ingredient,
  origin and net-weight curiosity is a genuine, repeated pattern in this log — roughly 250
  impressions of label-reading intent spread thin across many phrasings.
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25 — and the finding here is a negative one worth stating:
  - `https://food.chemlinked.com/foodpedia/labeling-requirement-in-malaysia` — the only Malaysia-
    specific result, and it is a **regulatory-compliance page written for manufacturers**, not for
    shoppers.
  - `https://sqcpenang.com/?p=2209` — same, a consultancy's guide to food labelling in Malaysia.
  - `https://www.fda.gov/files/food/published/PDF---Guidance-for-Industry--Proper-Labeling-of-Honey-and-Honey-Products.pdf` — US.
  - `https://honey.com/honey-industry/resources/honey-labeling` — US National Honey Board.
  - `https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/food-and-drink/labelling-of-honey` — UK.
  - `https://stompstickers.com/blogs/articles/whats-in-a-label-honey-label-requirements-made-easy` and `https://ctmlabelingsystems.com/a-complete-guide-to-honey-label-requirements/` — both are label-*printing* vendors selling to producers.

**What currently ranks**

**Nothing written for a Malaysian buyer.** The first page is split between US/UK regulators and
B2B compliance consultancies. Every single result addresses the person *printing* the label, not
the person *reading* it. That is an unusually clean gap — but see the risk register, because the
reason the gap exists is partly that the material is dry.

**The angle**

A shopper's translation of a compliance document: ingredient lists are ordered by proportion so the
first name is the bulk of the jar; a single-ingredient honey needs no ingredient list at all, so a
list appearing on a honey jar is itself information; net weight versus drained weight on a comb
product; who the named manufacturer or importer is and why an address matters more than a slogan;
what "pure", "natural", "premium" and "wild" legally guarantee (nothing); and the fact that
allergen declaration is mandatory — which is exactly why SUBUR's walnut and almond are named. That
last point is the article's own product tie-in and it costs nothing to make, because SUBUR's ten
ingredients are already published in full. It also gives `honey-allergy-symptoms` a natural inbound
link. The Arabic side already commissioned ingredient/label literacy in round 2 (a3); this is its
English sibling, which is an argument for grouping, not against.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* **low.** No health claim in the topic. **But** — FACTS-COMMON forbids statute
  numbers and regulation citations in body copy, and round 1 already got a sub-regulation number
  wrong once. That rule removes this article's strongest material. It can still say "Malaysian
  labelling rules require the ingredient list to be ordered by proportion" without a citation, but
  the writer must be briefed that they may describe the requirement and may never number it.
- *Fabrication:* **medium — the highest in the recommended six.** The temptation is to state a
  requirement more confidently than the sources support. Every "must" in this article has to trace
  to `food.chemlinked.com` or `sqcpenang.com`, both of which are secondary summaries, not the
  regulation. Anything either source does not cover should be written as "check" rather than
  "the law says". And: **no claim about SUBUR's own label** beyond the ingredient list already
  published on-site — no certification mark, no batch coding scheme, no net weight (no jar size
  is published anywhere).
- *Cannibalisation:* **medium.** Two neighbours. `how-to-identify-pure-honey` covers the jar's
  contents from the *authenticity* angle, and `is-malaysian-honey-local-or-imported` already lists
  `honey country of origin label` among its keywords. Boundary: this article is about **what the
  printed panel is required to tell you and what it is not**, and it must not re-argue authenticity
  or re-explain import origin. If the brief cannot hold that line cleanly, drop this candidate to
  reserve — it is the one recommended topic whose boundary is drawn by discipline rather than by
  subject matter.
- *Banned terms:* **low.**

**Cross-language:** Strong Malay (label copy in Malaysia is bilingual by law, which is itself
content). Arabic already has a3 from round 2 — check for overlap before grouping AR.
**EN + MS; AR only after an overlap check against a3.**

  - `https://homesteadersofamerica.com/the-different-colors-of-honey/`

**What currently ranks**

Mid-length US/AU editorial, 700–1,500 words, converging on one message: **dark = more antioxidants
= better.** They also list darker varietals — buckwheat, jarrah, chestnut, manuka — **none of
which is sold in Malaysia**, so a Malaysian reader gets no help identifying what is in front of

*darkens in storage* is a heat-exposure signal, not a floral one.

**The angle**

of what was added to it, not because of the flower.** That is literally SUBUR's case — black seed,
cinnamon, star anise, nutmeg and saffron pigment a blend far more than any nectar source does, and
a buyer comparing a spiced blend against a pale supermarket jar is not comparing grades at all. The
article separates three causes that look identical in a jar — floral source, added ingredients, and
heat damage in storage — and gives the reader the one honest discriminator (colour that *changed*
after purchase is the only one that means anything). Then it refuses the incumbents' payoff: darker
is not better, it is different, and colour is not a grade. Same refusal shape as
`how-to-identify-pure-honey` refusing the thumb test — which is why it fits the house voice.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* **medium.** The entire incumbent corpus resolves to an antioxidant claim. The

  paragraph is missing something, that missing thing is the illegal part.
- *Fabrication:* **low-medium.** No colour grade, Pfund reading or measurement exists for SUBUR and
  none may be invented. Say the blend is dark; do not quantify it.
- *Cannibalisation:* **low.** No live article covers colour. `types-of-honey-in-malaysia` mentions
  tualang pouring lighter and gelam sitting darker in passing, so the brief must keep this piece
  off varietal enumeration and link across instead.
- *Banned terms:* **low.**

**Cross-language:** Moderate Malay (`kenapa madu warna gelap`). Weak Arabic. **EN + MS.**

## Recommended top 6

**1. `is honey in malaysia fake`** — The best article available to a brand that publishes no
certifications, no lab results and no reviews, because its authority is borrowed entirely from
named public sources: a USM lecturer's claim and the Health Ministry's own contradicting monitoring
figure, both on the record, both dated. It converts ~420 impressions of "is this jar real"
verification intent into a piece with a genuine news hook, and it is the only candidate where the
absence of SUBUR's own data is not a weakness. Ship it first.

**2. `what is sidr honey`** — By a factor of ten the largest legal demand cluster in the GSC file,
sitting at position 11.5 and 14.4 — page two, meaning the audience is asking and nobody in the
retailer's orbit is answering. Zero cannibalisation: sidr and Yemen appear nowhere across the 16
live focus keywords. It is honest only if the article says outright that SUBUR is *not* sidr, and
that refusal is exactly the product-class argument `types-of-honey-in-malaysia` already makes
against kelulut and tualang — a settled house position extended to the honey the audience actually
types. Highest reward, and the one requiring the tightest claims brief.

**3. `can you put honey in hot tea`** — A dense international corpus proves the question is asked,
and not one result is Malaysian or addresses a jar with wax in it. The article gets to be metric,
teh-and-kopi shaped, and to end on advice that loses a sale: a comb blend is a spoon product, so
if you want honey in your drink this is not the jar. That refusal is the differentiator, and it is
the cheapest trust the round can buy.

**4. `baking with honey instead of sugar`** — The cleanest competitive gap in the file and the
lowest legal risk of anything here. Every incumbent is in cups and Fahrenheit; a Malaysian home
baker is doing two conversions before the advice works. Carries **zero GSC support** and is
recommended on SERP composition alone — stated plainly so the round map can weight it accordingly.
Same self-disqualifying honesty as #3, applied to the oven, plus the positive close (drizzle over,
don't bake in) that keeps it from being purely negative.

**5. `how to read a honey label`** — Every ranking result addresses the person *printing* the
label, not the person reading it. The shopper-facing version does not exist in Malaysia. It pays
SUBUR back directly, because mandatory allergen declaration is precisely why the walnut and almond
are named on a site that already publishes all ten ingredients — a real, checkable, unglamorous
trust signal. Recommended **conditionally**: the statute-citation ban removes its best material and
its boundary against `how-to-identify-pure-honey` is drawn by editorial discipline rather than by
subject. If the round is over-committed, this is the first of the six to cut.

**6. `why is my honey dark`** — The weakest GSC support of the six and honestly labelled as such
(~90 impressions, most of them a brand name rather than a colour question). It earns its place on
angle: it is the only candidate where the correct answer *is* SUBUR's own composition — a spiced
blend is dark because of black seed, cinnamon, star anise, nutmeg and saffron, not because of a
nectar source or a grade. Separating floral colour, added-ingredient colour and heat damage is
useful, un-written in Malaysia, and gives the reader the one discriminator that means anything
(colour that changed *after* purchase). Requires a flat refusal of the antioxidant payoff every
incumbent builds to.

**Grouping note for hreflang:** candidates 1 and 2 have strong demand in all three languages and
should be grouped EN/MS/AR. Candidates 3, 4, 5 and 6 group EN + MS. Candidate 5's Arabic slot
should only be filled after checking overlap with round 2's `a3` ingredient-literacy article.
