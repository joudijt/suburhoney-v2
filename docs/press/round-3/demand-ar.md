# Arabic demand research — PRESS round 3 (suburhoney.com)

**Researched:** 2026-08-25 · **Researcher:** Arabic demand agent, Stage 1 only.
**Method:** live Arabic-language SERP reads + Google Search Console rows from `gsc-demand.md`.
**Written in English with Arabic keywords inline** (round 2's file was written entirely in Arabic;
this round has a mixed-language review chain and a real GSC table to cross-reference, so the
structure is English and every keyword, quote and angle stays in Arabic).

## Read this before you read a single candidate

**No keyword-volume figure appears anywhere in this file.** There is no paid keyword tool on this
machine and the Cogny MCP quota is exhausted. Every number below is one of exactly two things,
and each row says which:

1. **A Search Console impression/position count** from `madinah.com.my` or `suburhoney.com`,
   quoted verbatim from `docs/press/round-3/gsc-demand.md`. These are real, measured, 16-month
   totals. `madinah.com.my` is the same retailer and the same customer mix, so it is evidence of
   **what this audience types** — never evidence that SUBUR is the answer to it.
2. **SERP composition** — what I saw with my own eyes on the Arabic results page on 2026-08-25:
   how many independent publishers compete, what depth they go to, whether any of them is written
   for someone standing in Malaysia. Publisher count is a *demand proxy*, not a volume figure.

Where a candidate has neither (a genuine content void), I say so and rank it accordingly rather
than inventing a reason to promote it.

**Audience:** Arabic speakers **living inside Malaysia** — Klang Valley residents, students,
expatriate families, Gulf/Levantine/Egyptian communities in KL and Selangor. Not Gulf shoppers.
Round 1 established this demand is store-led and practical, not brand-led. Round 3's GSC data
confirms it from a second direction: the sibling property's Arabic rows are verification questions
(`هل عسل الشفاء طبيعي`), and its highest-impression rows are **category** questions about honey
types, not brand-love queries.

**Product:** a raw honeycomb blend of ten named ingredients. **Not** Yemeni sidr, **not** Manuka,
**not** a royal-honey sachet. Walnuts and almonds are ground/blended in, never biteable pieces.
Comb (~0.95) and nuts (~1.0) float in honey (~1.4). Crystallisation is normal and proves nothing
on its own. MGO is not New Zealand-exclusive.

**Publishable facts:** two Selangor branches (Ampang Jaya, Batu Caves), a WhatsApp contact line,
a Shopee storefront, a TikTok Shop. **No price, no jar weight, no review, no rating, no customer
count, no certification, no bulk pathway, no map.**

## Candidate 1 — `الفرق بين عسل السدر والعسل الماليزي`

- **Focus keyword:** `الفرق بين عسل السدر والعسل الماليزي`
- **Slug:** `al-farq-bayn-asal-al-sidr-wal-asal-al-malizi`
- **Intent:** comparison / purchase orientation — a reader in an Arab grocery in Ampang holding a
  Yemeni sidr jar in one hand and a Malaysian honeycomb blend in the other, deciding which is
  "the real one."

**Demand evidence**

- GSC (`madinah.com.my`, 16 months, quoted from `gsc-demand.md`): `yemeni honey` — 1 click,
  **1,935 impressions**, avg position 11.5. `yemeni sidr honey` — 2 clicks, **1,801 impressions**,
  position 14.4. `yemeni honey ingredients` — 207 impressions, position 5.0. `sidr honey madinah`
  — 194 impressions, position 8.7. `honey sidr yemen` — 150 impressions, position 9.3.
  `sidr honey price` — 111 impressions, position 7.1. Twenty-plus further sidr/Yemeni rows below
  100 impressions each. Positions 8–14 on the big rows mean this audience is *seeing* the retailer
  and not clicking — the demand is measured and the answer is currently inadequate.
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25, query `الفرق بين عسل السدر اليمني والعسل الماليزي`: **not one result
  is about Malaysian honey.** Every returned page is a Gulf or Egyptian sidr seller comparing sidr
  to *other sidr* (Saudi, Kashmiri, Jordanian) or to "ordinary honey."
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25, query `عسل السدر اليمني في ماليزيا كوالالمبور شراء`: seven results,
  **zero of them located in Malaysia** — aalalkaif.com, janahoney.com, osaime.com, aseeleg.com,
  alshrhanhoney.com, alfakhirhoney.com, reeqalnahl.com. The word "ماليزيا" in the query changes
  nothing about what ranks. An Arabic speaker in KL asking this question is served Gulf shipping
  pages that do not ship to them.

**What currently ranks (top results, 2026-08-25)**

| # | URL | depth | why it fails an Arabic reader in Malaysia |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `store.alshifahoney.com/en/blog/الفرق-بين-عسل-السدر-وعسل-الزهور/a-1633185866` | seller blog, medium | Saudi seller; compares sidr to floral honey only; leans on benefit claims |
| 2 | `abounayefhoney.com/blog/أنواع-عسل-السدر/a-1397743738` | seller blog, medium-long | Compares Yemeni vs Saudi vs Kashmiri sidr — an intra-sidr taxonomy, no blend category at all |
| 3 | `alhawwaj.com/blog/الفرق-بين-عسل-السدر-والعسل-العادي/a-748214428` | seller blog, medium | "Ordinary honey" is the only comparator; therapeutic framing throughout |
| 4 | `alhussainattara.com/en/blog/-/a-1434184972` | thin | Uses/benefits listicle |
| 5 | `alowaidhoney.net/blogs/افضل-أنواع-العسل` | seller category guide | "Best honey types" ranking with a commercial answer baked in |

**The gap:** every ranking page treats "which honey is best" as a single ladder with sidr at the
top. Nobody explains that a **single-flora sourced honey** and a **blended honeycomb product** are
two different categories answering two different questions, and nobody writes it for someone whose
shelf is in Selangor.

**The angle.** Not "ours is better." A category explainer: sidr is honey from one named tree,
graded by origin and season, and priced on scarcity; SUBUR is raw honeycomb with ten named
ingredients blended in, priced and described on its ingredient list. A reader who wants a
single-flora honey should buy a single-flora honey — this page says so plainly, which is the whole
point of publishing it. What it adds that no competitor page can: the honest note that "Yemeni
sidr" is a **provenance claim you cannot verify from the jar**, and the same home tests the live
`kayfa-tamiz-al-asal-al-asli` article already demolished do not verify it either. Different from
the live Manuka article (`al-farq-bayn-asal-manuka-wal-asal-al-malizi`) because Manuka's comparator
is a **lab number** (MGO/UMF) while sidr's comparator is **provenance and floral source** — a
different axis, a different failure mode, a different reader worry.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* the entire ranking corpus attaches therapeutic claims to sidr (`خصائص طبية
  واسعة`, `تحسين الهضم`, `تعزيز جهاز المناعة` — seen verbatim in the 2026-08-25 SERP summary).
  The article must describe **origin, floral source, colour, aroma, texture, price mechanics** and
  nothing about the body — for sidr *or* for SUBUR. Do not repeat a competitor's health claim even
  to attribute it; naming it as "a claim commonly made" still publishes it.
- *Fabrication:* the retailer's GSC log shows it fields Yemeni-honey queries, but I have **not**
  confirmed from a publishable source that Berkat Madinah stocks Yemeni sidr. **Do not state or
  imply the store sells it.** Do not state a sidr price, a SUBUR price, or any comparative price
  language ("cheaper", "premium"). No jar weight — none is published anywhere in `src/config/`.
- *Cannibalisation:* nearest live neighbours are `al-farq-bayn-asal-manuka-wal-asal-al-malizi`
  (grade-based comparison — this piece must not re-argue MGO/UMF; one sentence of hand-off at most)
  and `al-asal-al-malizi` (which owns royal-honey sachets entirely — do not mention them here) and
  `kayfa-tamiz-al-asal-al-asli` (owns home authenticity tests — link, don't re-run them).
- *Banned terms:* high exposure. Sidr writing reaches reflexively for `تقليدي` and `عبر الأجيال`.
  Also watch the underlying claim behind `الشرق الأوسط` — do not substitute a regional adjective
  that carries the same framing (round 2 caught `شرق أوسطية` used exactly this way).

**hreflang:** EN plausibly shares this — GSC's `yemeni honey` (1,935) and `yemeni sidr honey`
(1,801) are Latin-script rows from the same property. MS shares it weakly (`madu yaman strong
honey`, 8 impressions). Flag as a **candidate group** for the EN researcher to confirm
independently; do not assume it.

## Candidate 2 — `هل يسمح بنقل العسل في الطائرة`

- **Focus keyword:** `هل يسمح بنقل العسل في الطائرة`
- **Slug:** `naql-al-asal-fi-al-taira`
- **Intent:** practical / logistics — a resident or student flying home for Eid or summer wants to
  carry a heavy glass jar of honeycomb blend out of KLIA without losing it at security.

**Demand evidence**

- No GSC row (this phrasing sits outside the retailer's catalogue footprint — expected).
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25, query `هل يسمح بنقل العسل في الطائرة حقيبة اليد سائل`: **six
  independent publishers competing**, including one page dedicated to exactly this question
  (`atyabtabkha.com/article/هل-العسل-ممنوع-في-الطائرة؟`) and two airline/airport authorities
  (`flynas.com/ar/baggage/prohibited-items`, `jordanaviation.jo/AR-article-2181`). A dedicated page
  from a mainstream Arabic food publisher plus airline pages competing on the same query is a
  reliable demand signal without a volume number.
- The recurring answer across all of them is the same three sentences — honey counts as a liquid,
  100 ml cabin limit, check your airline — and then they stop.

**What currently ranks (top results, 2026-08-25)**

| # | URL | depth | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `atyabtabkha.com/article/هل-العسل-ممنوع-في-الطائرة؟` | short, dedicated | Generic. No departure country, no packing method, no jar-type advice |
| 2 | `flynas.com/ar/baggage/prohibited-items` | authority list | A prohibited-items table, not an answer |
| 3 | `jordanaviation.jo/AR-article-2181` | authority list | Same shape, Jordan-specific |
| 4 | `sa.trip.com/guide/info/قيود-الأمتعة-المحمولة` | long guide | All liquids generically; honey is one bullet |
| 5 | `arabflying.com/السوائل-المسموح-بها-في-الطائرة` | medium | Liquids rules; nothing about food gifts |
| 6 | `traveldiv.com/قواعد-السوائل-المطار` | Q&A | Same |

**The gap:** nobody writes it from Malaysia, and nobody addresses the specific object — a **glass
jar containing solid comb and ground nuts**, which behaves differently from a squeeze bottle
(weight, breakage, checked-bag pressure changes, the comb layer shifting in transit).

**The angle.** A departure-side practical guide: cabin vs checked, why a comb jar goes in checked
baggage in practice, how to wrap glass so it survives a baggage belt, why the jar should be full
rather than half-empty, and the one honest caveat nobody states — **your destination's** customs
rules on bee products vary and this page will not pretend to know them; check before you fly.
Ends at the shop, not at a promise. Zero medical surface area, which makes it the lowest-legal-risk
candidate in this file.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* effectively nil. Nothing in this topic touches the body. This is why it ranks
  second despite thinner measured demand than candidate 3.
- *Fabrication:* real. Do **not** state a specific airline's honey policy as fact, do not state a
  gram or millilitre allowance as if it were universal, do not state a SUBUR jar weight (none
  published), do not state whether the shop packs for travel or ships internationally (not
  published — the live `shiraa-al-asal-online-fi-malizia` article already stops at "ask the shop"
  and this one must stop in the same place). The 100 ml cabin-liquids convention can be described
  as the widely applied rule, phrased as "check your airline and airport," never as a guarantee.
- *Cannibalisation:* low. No live article touches travel; `ayna-ashtari-asal-fi-kuala-lumpur`
  mentions `سفر` only in a passage about honeymoon-package SERP noise. Boundary vs candidate 6
  (gift-to-take-home) must be enforced: **this piece owns the logistics, candidate 6 owns the
  choice.** If both ship, they cross-link and neither repeats the other's core.
- *Banned terms:* low exposure.

**hreflang:** Arabic-specific in practice — the reader is someone whose family is abroad. EN could
share a thin version; MS almost certainly not (Malaysian residents are not the ones flying honey
out). Recommend **ungrouped**.

## Candidate 3 — `الفرق بين العسل الخام والعسل المصفى`

- **Focus keyword:** `الفرق بين العسل الخام والعسل المصفى`
- **Slug:** `al-farq-bayn-al-asal-al-kham-wal-musaffa`
- **Intent:** informational / trust — "raw" is on the label; what does it actually mean, and is it
  a regulated word or a marketing one?

**Demand evidence**

- GSC, adjacent rows on `madinah.com.my`: `black raw honey` 3 impressions, `where to buy pure
  honey` 5 impressions (1 click), `al shifa honey quality` 1 impression. Thin in Latin script —
  the demand here is evidenced by SERP composition, not by GSC.
- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25, query `الفرق بين العسل الخام والعسل المصفى المبستر`: **six independent
  publishers** competing, one of them the largest Arabic general-reference site (mawdoo3.com) and
  four of them dedicated seller explainers with the comparison in the title. Multiple dedicated
  pages on one comparison across independent publishers is a strong demand proxy.

**What currently ranks (top results, 2026-08-25)**

| # | URL | depth | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `frootsco.com/blogs/news/ما-هو-العسل-الخام-الغير-مبستر-وفوائده` | medium seller blog | Title literally ends in "وفوائده" — the whole page is a benefits argument |
| 2 | `mawdoo3.com/أنواع_العسل_وفوائده` | long reference | Types-and-benefits omnibus; the raw/processed line is a subsection |
| 3 | `store.alshifahoney.com/ar/blog/الفرق-بين-العسل-الخام-والعسل-المعالج/a-772147194` | "8 points" listicle | Seller with a stake; enzyme/antioxidant framing |
| 4 | `nasimalasal.com/blog/العسل-الحر/a-29696992` | medium | Regional term `العسل الحر`, benefits-led |
| 5 | `aryafalnahl.com/blog/الفرق-بين-العسل-الخام-والعسل-المعالج/a-1753221931` | medium | Same shape |
| 6 | `abounayefhoney.com/blog/الفرق-بين-العسل-الابيض-وعسل-النحل/a-2022760450` | "10 differences" | Different pair, same benefits engine |

Every one of them answers the process question and then immediately converts it into a health
argument — the SERP summary I pulled returns `يحتفظ بإنزيماته الطبيعية ومضادات الأكسدة ومغذياته`
almost verbatim from three different domains.

**The gap:** nobody separates the two halves. What heat and filtration physically do to honey
(texture, aroma, how fast it crystallises, whether pollen and wax fragments remain) is a
descriptive question with a real answer. Whether that changes anything in the body is a claim the
site cannot make. Splitting those two apart *is* the article, and no ranking page does it.

**The angle.** A process explainer with the claim layer deliberately cut out, plus the point no
seller wants to make: **"خام" is not a protected or regulated word** — anyone can print it. What
you can actually check is whether the jar still contains the things processing removes (visible
comb, sediment, cloudiness) and whether the ingredient list is complete. In a Malaysian climate the
practical consequence is real and local: an unpasteurised honey in a humid kitchen behaves
differently from a filtered supermarket bottle — and the page hands that off to the live
`hifz-al-asal-fi-al-jaw-al-ratb` article rather than re-running it.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* **high, and the reason this is #3 not #1.** The entire ranking corpus is built
  on enzymes/antioxidants/nutrients. Every one of those is a body claim. The article must describe
  only what happens to the honey itself. No "healthier", no "better for you", no "retains its
  goodness" — that last one is the trap phrasing that reads as harmless and is not.
- *Fabrication:* moderate. Do not state a pasteurisation temperature as if it were SUBUR's process
  spec (SUBUR's process is not published beyond "raw"). Do not claim a filtration standard, a lab
  test, or a certification. Do not imply the absence of certification is itself a quality signal.
- *Cannibalisation:* **real and must be managed.** `mukawwinat-khaltat-al-asal-bil-aashab` (ar3)
  carries `عسل خام أم عسل مصفى` as a *secondary* keyword — not a focus keyword, so this is not a
  hard collision under the round-3 rule, but it is the closest call in this file. Boundary: ar3
  owns **what is written on the label and why**; this piece owns **what the process physically
  does**. If the writer cannot hold that line in the outline, drop this candidate rather than ship
  a near-duplicate. Also hand off crystallisation to `hal-lil-asal-tarikh-salahiya` (which already
  covers it 12 times) — describe it in one sentence, link, move on.
- *Banned terms:* moderate. "Raw vs processed" writing pulls hard toward `تقليدي` and
  `عبر الأجيال` when describing pre-industrial extraction. Avoid the frame entirely; describe
  equipment, not eras.

**hreflang:** EN and MS both plausibly share (`madu mentah` is a live shelf term in Malaysia). Flag
as a **candidate group** but require independent research on each side — do not translate.

## Candidate 4 — `قراءة ملصق العسل في ماليزيا`

- **Focus keyword:** `قراءة ملصق العسل في ماليزيا`
- **Slug:** `qiraat-mulsaq-al-asal-fi-malizia`
- **Intent:** practical / trust — an Arabic speaker in a Malaysian shop is holding a jar whose
  label is in Malay and cannot read a word of it.

**Demand evidence**

- **This is a content void, and I am ranking it on that basis honestly, not pretending it has
  measured volume.** SERP, pulled 2026-08-25, query `مصطلحات ماليزية على المنتجات madu asli معنى
  بالعربي ملصق`: the results are a dictionary entry (`almaany.com/ar/dict/ar-id/madu`), a generic
  Arabic food-label article (`webteb.com/articles/بطاقات-العناصر-الغذائية_16198`), an Arabic
  dictionary definition of the word "ملصق", an English packaging-compliance glossary, and three
  Wikipedia/culture-trip pages about the Malay language. **Not one page connects Malay honey-label
  vocabulary to an Arabic reader.**
- The indirect demand signal is GSC and is real: `madu al shifa palsu` (30 impressions), `perbedaan
  madu al shifa asli dan palsu` (18), `madu al shifa asli dan palsu` (9), `alshifa madu` (6),
  `al shifa madu` (2), `al syifa madu` (1), `madu madinah` (155), `madu yaman strong honey` (8).
  This audience is **already typing Malay honey words into search** — which is exactly the reader
  who cannot read the jar and is guessing.

**What currently ranks:** nothing on-topic. The nearest competitor is a bilingual dictionary entry.
This is the only candidate in the file with no genuine competitor to describe.

**The angle.** A short Malay→Arabic glossary of what is actually printed on a honey jar in
Malaysia — `madu`, `asli`, `mentah`, `tulen`, `campuran`, `tanpa gula tambahan`, `berat bersih`,
`tarikh luput`, `kelulut`, `tualang` — with, for each one, the honest note on what the word does
and does not commit the seller to. Two of those words carry the article's real payload:
`asli`/`tulen` are marketing words with no regulated meaning, and `tarikh luput` on a honey jar
means something different from what it means on milk (handed off to the live
`hal-lil-asal-tarikh-salahiya` article rather than re-argued). Turns the site's blank
certification/ratings fields into the point of the page: what is written down is what you can
check.

**Risk register**

- *Legal ceiling:* very low. Vocabulary and label mechanics only. The one live wire is
  `tanpa gula tambahan` — describe it as a label statement about composition, never as a
  nutritional or dietary benefit.
- *Fabrication:* the discipline here is not inventing a **Malaysian labelling requirement**. Do not
  state that any term is legally mandated or legally defined unless the writer can cite a real
  source, and do not cite regulation numbers in body copy (site rule). The safe formulation is
  descriptive: "you will see this word; here is what it tells you and what it doesn't." Also: do
  not describe SUBUR's own label field-by-field beyond what `src/config/site.ts` publishes — no
  weight, no batch code, no certification mark.
- *Cannibalisation:* moderate against `mukawwinat-khaltat-al-asal-bil-aashab` (ar3), which owns
  ingredient-list literacy. Boundary: ar3 = **the ingredient list**; this = **the Malay words
  around it**. Also do not re-explain expiry dates (`hal-lil-asal-tarikh-salahiya`) or authenticity
  tests (`kayfa-tamiz-al-asal-al-asli`); link both.
- *Banned terms:* low.

**hreflang:** **Arabic-only by construction.** A Malay reader does not need a Malay glossary; an
English reader needs a different one. Do not group.

## Candidate 5 — `طريقة عمل عسل بالمكسرات في البيت`

- **Focus keyword:** `طريقة عمل عسل بالمكسرات`
- **Slug:** `tariqat-amal-asal-bil-mukassarat`
- **Intent:** how-to, with a buried purchase decision — the reader wants to make a nut-honey blend
  at home, which is a decision about whether to make it or buy it.

**Demand evidence**

- SERP, pulled 2026-08-25, query `عسل بالمكسرات خلطة عسل بالجوز واللوز طريقة`: **six results, and
  the intent is overwhelmingly DIY** — `almodawy.com/طريقة-عمل-عسل-بالمكسرات-وفوائده`,
  `attar-knooz.com/blog/عسل-بالمكسرات/a-1607407641` ("طرق تحضيره واستخداماته"),
  `nectar-honey.com/ar/AzlmAXA` ("خلطه عسل بالمكسرات"), plus two product listings
  (`honey-drop-store.com`, `shanahoney.net`) and one intimacy-claim page
  (`alriyfalnajdiu.com/blogs/خلطة-العسل-والمكسرات-للجنس`).
- Second SERP, 2026-08-25, query `هل تفسد المكسرات داخل العسل زنخ الجوز واللوز`: results are a
  medical Q&A site, a Masrawy piece on nuts going rancid generally
  (`masrawy.com/.../3036181/...`), a Facebook post, and two benefits pages. Nobody joins nuts,
  rancidity and honey together.
- The Arabic demand shape here is **materially different from the English round's**, which is why
  this is a candidate at all — see the cannibalisation note.

**What currently ranks (top results, 2026-08-25)**

| # | URL | depth | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `almodawy.com/طريقة-عمل-عسل-بالمكسرات-وفوائده/` | recipe + benefits | Recipe then benefits; no failure modes |
| 2 | `attar-knooz.com/blog/عسل-بالمكسرات/a-1607407641` | seller blog | Preparation and uses, seller-led |
| 3 | `nectar-honey.com/ar/AzlmAXA` | thin | Recipe stub |
| 4 | `honey-drop-store.com/products/خلطة-عسل-خاصة-بالمكسرات-12-كيلو` | product listing | Listing, not content |
| 5 | `alriyfalnajdiu.com/blogs/خلطة-العسل-والمكسرات-للجنس` | claim page | Direct ceiling violation as a model |

Every recipe uses **whole nut pieces** ("قطع المكسرات بعسل النحل بملعقة") and every one of them
skips what actually goes wrong: fresh nuts carry moisture, moisture in honey is what allows
fermentation, and a home kitchen cannot grind nuts to the texture a blended product has.

**The angle.** Give the honest recipe — this is a house-voice move, the same one
`kayfa-tamiz-al-asal-al-asli` makes when it tells readers their home tests do not work — and then
give the three things the recipes omit: moisture and fermentation risk in a humid Malaysian
kitchen, the difference between nuts you bite and nuts blended in, and the fact that a home mix
has a shorter usable life than the honey did on its own. Ends by describing what a ready blend
does differently, without a price or a superiority claim.

**Risk register**
