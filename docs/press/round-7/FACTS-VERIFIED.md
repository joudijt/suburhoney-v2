## 1. Malaysian claim rules — the backbone of the `tenaga batin` and `royal honey` clusters

### The food-label claims rule

The Food Regulations 1985 say, verbatim (English text hosted by MOH):

> "No label which describes any food shall include any claim— … (c) which cannot be substantiated;
> (d) as to the suitability of a food for use in the prevention, alleviation, treatment or cure of
> a disease, disorder or particular physiological condition, except as otherwise permitted in these
> Regulations; or (e) which could give rise to doubt about the safety of a similar food or arouse or
> exploit fear in the consumer."

— SOURCE: https://hq.moh.gov.my/fsq/xs/dl.php?filename=Food+Regulation+1985+Update+291024+%28PUA+209_2020%29.pdf `[REGULATOR]`

MOH's own plain-language version: *"Produk makanan adalah dilarang untuk membuat sebarang tuntutan
perubatan."* — SOURCE: https://pharmacy.moh.gov.my/en/faq-page/10-0 `[REGULATOR]`

⛔ **Refer to this in words only.** No regulation number, no sub-regulation number, no penalty
figure. Three earlier rounds got a sub-regulation number wrong and one invented an RM figure.

### The label rule reaches advertising

The Food Act 1983 makes it an offence to publish an advertisement for a food that *"directly or
indirectly qualifies or is inconsistent with or contrary to any particulars required by
regulations"* or that *"is prohibited by any such regulations from being marked on or attached to
such food"*. In plain terms: **what may not go on the label may not go in the ad.**
— SOURCE: https://hq.moh.gov.my/fsq/xs/dl.php?filename=Food+Act+1983.pdf `[REGULATOR]`

### ⭐ The single most important fact for the `tenaga batin` cluster

The **Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act 1956** says no person shall take part in publishing an
advertisement referring to **any article** in terms *"calculated to lead to the use of that article
… as a medicine, an appliance or a remedy"* for the purposes it lists, which include *"improving
the sexual function or sexual performance of human beings"*. Its Schedule of conditions includes
**infertility**, **frigidity**, **impairment of sexual function or impotency**, and *"nervous
debility, or other complaint or infirmity, arising from or relating to sexual intercourse"*.

— SOURCE: https://pharmacy.moh.gov.my/sites/default/files/document-upload/medicine-advertisement-sale-act-1956-act-290_1.pdf `[REGULATOR]`

⚠️ **This Act bites on *any article* advertised as a remedy — it is not limited to registered
medicines.** That is why a food sold in Malaysia cannot advertise a `tenaga batin` outcome. Name
the Act in words. **No section numbers. No penalties.**

### Food vs registered traditional product — the distinction two articles are built on

| | Food | Registered traditional product |
|---|---|---|
| Regulator | Food Safety and Quality Division (FSQD), MOH | National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency (NPRA) |
| Registration number | **None. A food is not supposed to have one** | `MAL` + 8 digits + a letter code |
| What triggers it | *"100% food ingredients"*, *"Products in conventional food form"*, *"Herbs and spices in crude form without medicinal/health claim"* | an *"active ingredient(s)"*, a *"pharmaceutical dosage form such as soft gel, capsule or tablet"*, or a *"medicinal/health claim"* |

NPRA's own framing: *"This guide serves to assist in determining if a product is to be regulated by
the National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Division (NPRA) or by the Food Safety and Quality Division
(FSQD)."* Products that straddle the line are called **Food-Drug Interphase (FDI)**: *"products with
combination of food ingredients and active ingredients for oral consumption."*
— SOURCE: https://www.npra.gov.my/index.php/en/classification-guideline/product-classification-guideline.html `[REGULATOR]`

MOH: *"Produk makanan dan minuman tidak perlu berdaftar dengan KKM."*
— SOURCE: https://pharmacy.moh.gov.my/en/faq-page/10-0 `[REGULATOR]`

⛔ **SUBUR is a food.** It has no MAL number and is not supposed to have one. Never imply it is
registered, was tested, or would pass anything — and never imply that having no MAL number is a
defect. Both directions are wrong.

### The MAL number, and how a consumer checks one

MOH's wording: *"Nombor pendaftaran MAL dimulakan dengan 'MAL', diikuti dengan 8 angka, dan diakhiri
dengan kod produk."* Example form: `MAL12345678X`. The letter codes:

| Code | Meaning (MOH's words) |
|---|---|
| `A` | *"Ubat terkawal (Ubat preskripsi)"* |
| `X` | *"Ubat terkawal tanpa preskripsi atau ubat over-the-counter (OTC)"* |
| `T` | *"Ubat tradisional"* |
| `N` | *"Suplemen kesihatan"* |

Registered products also carry a **FarmaTag™ hologram**, checkable with the free FarmaTag Checker
app. — SOURCE: https://pharmacy.moh.gov.my/ms/soalan-lazim/bagaimana-mengenal-pasti-ubat-ubatan-berdaftar.html `[REGULATOR]`

**The public lookup tool** is NPRA's **QUEST3+ Product Search** — *"Carian Produk Berdaftar /
Bernotifikasi"* — at **https://quest3plus.bpfk.gov.my/pmo2/index.php**, linked from
https://www.npra.gov.my/index.php/my/consumers-2/maklumat/carian-produk-berdaftar-bernotifikasi.html `[REGULATOR]`

**Advertising approval is a separate number.** An advertisement approved by the Medicine
Advertisements Board carries a **KKLIU** number: *"The approval number begins with KKLIU, followed by
4 digits referring to the serial number and ends with year"* (form: `KKLIU 1234/2018`). Only health
supplements (N), traditional medicines (T) and OTC medicines (X) need that approval. **KKLIU is on
the advertisement; MAL is on the product.**
— SOURCE: https://pharmacy.moh.gov.my/en/content/how-recognize-approved-advertisement.html `[REGULATOR]`

### Laws by name only — never a section number, never a penalty

Food Act 1983 · Food Regulations 1985 · Poisons Act 1952 (this is what makes something a *racun
berjadual*) · Sale of Drugs Act 1952 · Control of Drugs and Cosmetics Regulations 1984 ·
Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act 1956. `[REGULATOR]`

The Food Act 1983 makes it an offence to prepare or sell food *"that has in or upon it any substance
which is poisonous, harmful or otherwise injurious to health"*.
— SOURCE: https://hq.moh.gov.my/fsq/xs/dl.php?filename=Food+Act+1983.pdf `[REGULATOR]`

### Nutrient function claims — permitted, but a closed list, and honey is not on it

> "'nutrient function claim' means a nutrition claim that describes the physiological role of the
> nutrient in the growth, development and normal functions of the body."
> "A nutrient function claim shall not imply or include any statement to the effect that the
> nutrient would afford a cure or treatment for or protection from a disease."
> "Except as otherwise provided in these Regulations, only the following nutrient function claims or
> any other words of similar meaning shall be permitted:" — followed by an enumerated list (folic
> acid, sialic acid, iron, inulin/oligofructose and others).
> "No label on a package containing any food shall bear other function claim except those claims
> permitted in these Regulations or with prior written approval of the Director."

— SOURCE: Food Regulations 1985 PDF above `[REGULATOR]`. ⚠️ **Honey is not on the permitted list.**

## 3. Honeycomb and bee products

- **Comb honey** *"contains newly built wax comb filled with honey, just as honey bees naturally
  store it."* Producing it means keeping *"highly populated colonies within a more confined space
  than would be typical"*, which makes them *"more prone to swarming."*
  — SOURCE: https://extensionpubs.unl.edu/publication/g2362/2025/pdf/view/g2362-2025.pdf `[STUDY — university extension]`
- **Cappings** are the wax seal bees put over ripened honey. *"Any cells not capped by the bees
  before harvest do not contain shelf stable honey and should not be included… Shelf stable honey
  has been dehydrated by the bees to a low moisture content (< 18%) which prevents fermentation."*
  — SOURCE: same `[STUDY]`
- **Harvesting comb does not kill the colony.** Bees are cleared with *"a soft bristled bee brush to
  gently brush the bees into the open brood chamber."* The Langstroth hive *"features
  interchangeable boxes (supers) with removable frames (combs) that allow for colony inspection,
  pest/disease treatment, and honey collection without destroying the colony."*
  — SOURCES: same + https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN1005 `[STUDY]`
- **But comb costs the colony more than liquid honey does.** *"Honey bees require approximately 8
  pounds of honey to produce 1 pound of beeswax. Since none of the wax is returned to the bees, it is
  more energetically demanding for the bees."* ⚠️ The underlying experiment gave a **range of
  6.6–8.8 lb**. Write the range, or write "roughly six to nine pounds". **Never "exactly 8".**
  — SOURCES: same + https://scientificbeekeeping.com/light-or-heavy-syrup-for-drawing-foundation/ `[STUDY]`/`[TRADE]`
- **FAO definitions** — use these verbatim or close to it:
  - honeycomb: *"a small section of completely sealed comb built of virgin (new) beeswax"*
  - beeswax: secreted from *"8 wax glands on the underside of the abdomens of the young bees"*;
    composition roughly monoesters ~35%, hydrocarbons ~14%, diesters ~14%, free acids ~12%,
    hydroxy polyesters ~8%
  - propolis: *"a mixture of various amounts of beeswax and resins collected by the honeybee from
    plants"*
  - royal jelly: *"secreted by the hypopharyngeal gland … of young worker (nurse) bees"*, and
    *"always fed directly to the queen or the larvae as it is secreted; it is not stored."*
  — SOURCES: https://www.fao.org/4/w0076e/w0076e07.htm · w0076e12.htm · w0076e14.htm · w0076e16.htm `[REGULATOR — UN/FAO]`
- **US legal definition of beeswax**: *"Beeswax (CAS Reg. No. 8012-89-3) is a secretory product of
  honey bees used as a structural material in honeycombs."* GRAS-affirmed *"at levels not to exceed
  good manufacturing practice."* — SOURCE: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/184.1973 `[REGULATOR]`
- **Digestibility — do NOT overclaim.** JECFA: *"It is generally believed that waxes are not digested
  or absorbed from the alimentary tract in most mammals, including man… **There are no original
  research data available to support this claim.**"* JECFA also *"noted that beeswax might have
  allergenic potential and that the consumer should be made aware of its presence in foods."*
  The extension service's practical version: *"The beeswax comb itself is indigestible but harmless
  and can be eaten along with the honey or spat out."*
  — SOURCES: https://www.inchem.org/documents/jecfa/jecmono/v30je11.htm + UNL g2362 `[REGULATOR]`/`[STUDY]`
  ⚠️ EFSA did **not** set an ADI — it found the data insufficient and concluded the uses are *"not of
  safety concern"*. Never write "EFSA approved a safe daily limit". `[REGULATOR — partially verified]`
- **Cutting and serving.** Tools named by the extension service: *"a very thin paring knife"*, a
  cookie sheet, a cooling rack. Temperature: *"cold wax is brittle… thaw frozen frames completely
  before cutting and packaging"*, and *"Comb honey cannot be heated otherwise comb will melt."* FAO:
  *"If left in the sun even momentarily, wax cappings will become transparent and the comb will break
  easily."* ⛔ **The cheese-wire trick is hobbyist folklore with no authority behind it — do not
  present it as guidance.** There is **no sourced numeric serving temperature**: write "room
  temperature, never chilled, never heated".
  — SOURCES: UNL g2362 + https://www.fao.org/4/w0076e/w0076e07.htm `[STUDY]`/`[REGULATOR]`
- **Comb honey does crystallise.** Freezing only *"may also slow crystallization"*. Never write that
  comb honey does not crystallise. — SOURCE: UNL g2362 `[STUDY]`

## 4. "Madu asli", authenticity and Malaysian honey standards

### What the words actually mean on a label

**"Pure" / "tulen" IS restricted.** Food Regulations 1985, verbatim:

> "No label which describes any food shall include the word 'pure' or any other words of the same
> significance unless— (a) the food is of the strength, purity, or quality prescribed by these
> Regulations and is free from any other added substance apart from those essential in the processing
> of such food; and (b) there is no expressed stipulation in these Regulations prohibiting the
> inclusion of such word in the label in respect of that food."

The Malay text of the same rule uses **"tulen"**.
— SOURCES: the Food Regulations 1985 PDF above + https://hq.moh.gov.my/fsq/xs/dl.php?filename=b13cc3e54220a94b8826745d2e74b930.pdf `[REGULATOR]`

**"Asli" and "original" are named nowhere.** They would only be caught by the catch-all *"apa-apa
perkataan lain yang mempunyai makna yang sama"* — a reading MOH has not published. So:

- ✅ safe: *"asli is a marketing word, not a defined one; the word the regulation actually names is
  a different one, and it is restricted to foods with nothing added beyond what processing requires."*
- ⛔ not safe: *"asli is legally defined"*, or applying the purity rule to any named product.

The same regulation also **forbids the words "compounded", "medicated", "tonic" and "health" or any
words of the same significance** on a food label, and restricts grading or superiority words unless
they conform to a description established by the relevant authority. `[REGULATOR]`

### Malaysia's own definition of honey

Food Regulations 1985: *"Honey shall be the sugary product obtained from the comb of the honey
bee."* — followed by compositional limits (reducing sugars, water, ash, HMF, apparent sucrose,
acidity). **The regulation contains no "may contain" clause: it authorises no additions.** Verified
directly against the MOH-hosted English PDF.
— SOURCE: https://hq.moh.gov.my/fsq/xs/dl.php?filename=Food+Regulation+1985+Update+291024+%28PUA+209_2020%29.pdf `[REGULATOR]`

⚠️ The compositional numbers are already published, correctly, in the live article
`en/is-honey-in-malaysia-fake` (*"at least 60 per cent reducing sugars, no more than 10 per cent…"*).
**Do not restate them — link to that article.**

### Codex

**CXS 12-1981, Standard for Honey** — adopted 1981, revised 1987 and 2001. Definition, verbatim:

> "Honey is the natural sweet substance produced by honey bees from the nectar of plants or from
> secretions of living parts of plants or excretions of plant sucking insects on the living parts of
> plants, which the bees collect, transform by combining with specific substances of their own,
> deposit, dehydrate, store and leave in the honey comb to ripen and mature."

§3.1: *"Honey sold as such shall not have added to it any food ingredient, including food additives,
nor shall any other additions be made other than honey."* §6.1.1: *"Products conforming to Part One
of the Standard shall be designated 'honey'."* The only blend Codex recognises is honey-with-honey
(§6.1.4, *"a blend of honeydew honey with blossom honey"*). **Codex has no category for honey
blended with nuts, spices or herbs.**
— SOURCE: https://www.fao.org/input/download/standards/310/cxs_012e.pdf `[REGULATOR]`

⛔ See trap 3. State the Codex rule; do not convert it into a Malaysian legal verdict, and do not
audit any named product — SUBUR included — against it.

### Malaysian Standards

| Standard | Title | Status |

| **MS 2683:2025** | *Dehydrated kelulut (stingless bee) honey — Specification* | 1st revision, supersedes MS 2683:2017 |

methods, hygiene, packaging and labelling, for honey … produced by honey bees of Apis sp. intended

*"STANDARDS MALAYSIA has appointed SIRIM Berhad as the agent to develop, distribute and sell
